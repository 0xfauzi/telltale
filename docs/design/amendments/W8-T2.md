# W8-T2: uncaptured change rows, and the delayed label moved to where it is decidable

Amends design 6.12, "Change clock". Five changes to the clock, one statement of a defect
the clock does not fix, and one module split.

## 1. A commit no capture landed is a row

6.12 built the change clock out of commits linked to a capture, so the lineage it
produced was the set of commits somebody happened to record a session for. That is a
sample of the repository chosen by the tooling. Measured on 2026-09-06: this repository
has 158 first-parent commits (`git rev-list --first-parent --count HEAD`), and E11
reported the change clock of it holding 40 rows on the owner's store of 2026-09-03.

`link_confidence: "unlinked"` is now a rung the clock accepts. It is NOT on spec 12.3's
ladder, because it is not a link, and it is not one of the two low-confidence rungs,
because nothing about it was guessed: it is a recorder saying "I read the history and I
made no claim about which session produced this". A change at that rung that no attempt
landed carries the row flag `uncaptured`.

A sha recorded by BOTH a launcher capture and a history backfill is one row, at the
launcher's rung, with the launcher's process columns and no flag. `series_changes._rung`
prefers any ladder word over `unlinked` whoever wrote it, and the payload the row reads
is the one recorded AT the winning rung (`_at_rung`), so a build of one store is the
same row twice running. Duplicate is not one.

## 2. What an uncaptured row holds, and what it says about what it does not

The six repository columns (`files_changed`, `lines_added`, `lines_removed`,
`subsystems_touched`, `test_files_changed`, `dependency_delta`) are read off the
`telltale.repo.commit` payload exactly as on a landed row.

The seven process columns (`fresh_input_tokens_total`, `cache_read_tokens_total`,
`compactions`, `verification_cycles`, `edit_turnover_ratio`, `stable_state_intervals`,
`unique_files_read`) are None, and `attempts_to_land` is None. They were already None by
arithmetic (a sum over no rows is unknown, a ratio over no edits is undefined). What
W8-T2 adds is the sentence, one per column, in the cohort's `unknown_columns`:

> no capture landed this change: the row is a repository fact and the process that
> produced it was not observed

The environment fingerprint is None too, because a fingerprint comes from the landing
attempts. On a frame of only uncaptured rows `series.env_column` therefore returns
`unavailable` and every cell of `env_changed` is None. That is env_column's own rule
over the row fingerprints and not `_measured`: `_specs` exempts `env_changed` from the
cell rule and takes the word env_column returned. Measured on this repository's 158-row
backfilled lineage: `env_changed  flag  past_covariate  unavailable  158`.

### The coverage floor

Six of those seven columns rest on a provider capability (`request_usage`,
`compaction`, `commands`, `file_paths`), and a capability map is measured PER CAPTURE.
An uncaptured row belongs to no capture the map describes, so on a frame holding one
the capability word overstates the column: before this amendment a mixed frame reported
`fresh_input_tokens_total` as `observed` with seven of eight cells empty, and a
`--policy refuse` build of it would have refused over a hole no capture could ever fill.

The rule now: on the change clock, and only when the frame holds an uncaptured row, a
capability-backed process column's word is the WORSE of its capability word and the word
its own cells earn (`series_lineage._specs`, parameter `floor`). It only ever weakens.
On a frame of landed changes alone nothing changes, so `refuse` keeps its teeth against
a hole in a column a capture could see.

## 3. Post-merge columns read outcomes that name the sha

`series_outcomes.columns` took the outcomes of the attempts that landed the change and
the identities those attempts carried. An uncaptured change has neither. It now also
takes the activities of the capture that RECORDED the commit, and reads the outcomes
among them whose `component_id` is the sha (`series_outcomes.about`).

`merge_verification_ms` and `merge_verification_failed` come off the last such
`mechanical_verification` exactly as for a landed change. `rework_within_3` counts a
`revert_or_repair` naming the sha when its timestamp is at or before the deadline, which
is unchanged: the commit time of the third following change, None on the last three rows
of any lineage with NO_TAIL.

Two details are decisions rather than translations.

  A backfilled outcome is dated by its `timestamp` FIELD, not by its activity position.
  A posted outcome's position is when the receiver stamped it, and `telltale outcome`
  sets the payload timestamp to now, so the two agree. A backfilled one does not: the
  import ran today about a commit that landed months ago. An outcome with no readable
  timestamp leaves the cell None with UNREADABLE_TIMESTAMP rather than being dated.

  0 for "no rework outcome" rests on there being a name an event COULD have carried: an
  attempt identity on the captured path, the sha on the backfilled one. With neither,
  nothing was looked for, and NO_IDENTITY says so and the cell is None.

An uncaptured row's `activities` now include those sha-named outcomes, so they are in
its provenance and its `row_end_ts` moves to cover them. A rework outcome is stamped at
the REWORKING commit's time, which is later than the row's own commit: the label was not
decidable before then, and `row_end_ts` says when a row closed rather than when its
commit landed.

## 4. `rework_within_3_lag3`, and the look-ahead inside a context

One column is appended to the change clock, immediately after `rework_within_3` and
before `env_changed`:

    rework_within_3_lag3   flag   row j holds change j - 3's rework_within_3

**The defect it works around, stated plainly.** `rework_within_3` on row j is decided by
outcomes observed up to the commit time of change j + 3. `forecast/candidate.py` caps
origins at `o <= N - 3`, which protects the ACTUAL side only. In a backtest at origin o
the context rows j in [o - 3, o) therefore carry labels decided by commits at index >= o.
That is look-ahead inside the context. It has been invisible so far because E11 planned
0 windows on this repository and every label it could have read was 0. W8-T2 does not
change the compiler's cells: `rework_within_3` still says what it has always said, and
the row it says it about is still the row it is about.

**Why the lagged column is not look-ahead.** Row j's cell is change j - 3's label, and
that label's window closes at change j's own commit time, because `deadline(j - 3)` IS
change j's commit time. So the cell is decided by the moment row j closes. A context
ending at origin o holds rows 0..o-1, and every cell of this column in it was decided by
a commit at index <= o - 1. Nothing after the origin. `series check` tests the claim
rather than taking it: row j's provenance is extended with row j - 3's activity ids, and
the no-look-ahead check resolves every one of them against row j's `row_end_ts`.

**How E16 reads it.** E16 forecasts this column at H = 4 through a lagged frame built
from the Series, so that the fourth step at origin o is change o's own label. That is
the pre-registered way round the defect above, and it is the coordinator's experiment,
not this task's.

The first three rows of any lineage have no source change and are None, as is any row
whose source label is None; the cohort carries NO_LAG for both.

## 5. Cohort

`providers` now includes the provider of any capture that carried a row without being an
attempt, so a git-backfilled lineage reads `["git"]` and a mixed one `["claude", "git"]`.
`captured_rows` and `uncaptured_rows` are new keys, so two frames of one repository that
differ only in how many rows are backfilled are two series ids. Both are counted AFTER
the regime cut of spec 14.6, off the row flags rather than off the change list: a
`--regime pre` frame is a slice of the rows, and a count taken before the slice would
describe a frame the reader was not handed.

## 6. What a reader of a stored run sees

An `uncaptured` row is not excluded from a backtest. It is a row whose process columns
are unavailable, and W7-T3's rule already excludes an unavailable COLUMN by name and
records it in the run's warnings. `backtest.run` now splices in one
sentence from `series_lineage.uncaptured` ("N of M rows are uncaptured: their process columns are unavailable and were
excluded by name"), and `forecast readiness` prints `uncaptured N` beside the row count
on its header line, so a reader who sees seven columns named as not forecastable is told
the cause and not only the symptom.

Measured on this repository's backfilled lineage (158 rows, synthetic check runs so the
target is observed): check 1 FAILS naming all fourteen weak columns, check 2 plans 142
windows against k_min 20, and the remaining six pass.

## 7. Module layout

The change clock moved from `series_lineage.py` to `series_changes.py`. That file was at
the 800-line ratchet and this work added 130 lines to it; the cut is the third the
ratchet has forced, after `series_paths.py` and `series_outcomes.py`. `series_lineage.py`
keeps the lineage reader, the attempt clock and everything both clocks count, and
`build()` dispatches to `series_changes.change_series` through a local import for the
reason `series.build` imports `series_lineage` locally: the two share a vocabulary and a
top-level import would be a cycle.
