# E07: which request-clock targets of this build's own sessions can be forecast at all, and does TimesFM-3 beat the baselines on any of them?

Why this matters (30 seconds): everything the forecasting laboratory can ever say rests
on a comparison nobody has made on real data. W1-T6 scored the backtester on a synthetic
random walk, W2-T5 built the readiness checklist against synthetic series, and W2-T7
found and removed the encoding defect that had been dropping every window of every real
capture. Three claims depend on this run. That a request clock built from a real session
passes the checklist at all, because a laboratory whose preflight refuses every real
input is a laboratory that has never run. That the registry's three targets are targets
on this provider, because two of them may not vary at all in a Claude session. And that
TimesFM-3 earns its place beside four one-line baselines, because if it does not, every
forecast this system offers is a rolling median with a licence attached.

## Decision rule, written before the run

Copied verbatim from the W2-E07 brief, which was written before `experiments/E07/run.py`
existed and before any capture had been scored.

> The decision rule (design 6.12), and what half of it is missing. Per (target, H,
> capture): E_M = mean MAE of timesfm in true order, E_B = min over the four baselines,
> W_MB = share of windows where timesfm beats the best baseline; baseline sufficient
> iff E_M > (1 - delta) E_B or W_MB < w, with delta 0.10 and w 0.60. The placebo half
> (E_P, W_MP, the two-halves clause) needs block_shuffle, which is W3-T2 and does not
> exist: this experiment can label "baseline sufficient" or "not baseline sufficient at
> this n, placebo pending", never "temporal evolution" or "conditional prediction".
> Print the inequalities with their values beside every label. Constants printed in
> every output: delta 0.10, w 0.60, k_min 20, c_min 32, H in {1, 4}, stride H, baseline
> window 8, tau q80 of the first c_min rows, relative resolution 0.25.

Two further rules came with it and are followed here. A capture that fails readiness is
excluded and listed with the check that failed, never admitted by moving a constant. And
"fewer than three captures pass" is a valid outcome that gets written up as such: it is
the outcome for two of the three targets below.

## What we did

Apple M5, macOS 26.6.2 (Darwin 25.6.0), arm64. CPython 3.12.8, uv 0.11.29, torch 2.13.0,
timesfm 3.0.0, checkpoint `google/timesfm-3.0-pytorch` from the Hugging Face default
cache. No agent session was started and no token was spent: every number below comes from
sessions that had already been recorded.

```
uv sync --extra forecast
uv run --extra forecast python experiments/E07/run.py
uv run python experiments/E07/aggregate.py
uv run python experiments/E07/check_numbers.py
```

`aggregate.py` re-runs no forecast: it reads `out/summary.json` and the stored windows
and writes `out/aggregates.json`, so that every cross-run statistic quoted below (a
median skill, a flag count, a share of quantile points) sits in a file rather than only
in this prose. `check_numbers.py` then reads every number out of this document and asks
whether anything in `out/` matches it at the precision it was written to: **4,036 of
4,048 match**, and the twelve that do not are the three version strings in the paragraph
above, five tokens of E03's measurements cited as E03's, two hyphens in "central-80" read
as minus signs, and the two counts in this sentence.

The second command is the experiment: 120.665 s of wall time, exit 0. It copies the
owner's store, rebuilds the copy, enumerates the captures, builds one request-clock series per
build capture, runs the eight readiness checks for each of the three registry targets at
H = 1 and H = 4, backtests every pair that passes with the five default forecasters plus
TimesFM-3, persists each run through `store.put_forecast_run`, reads it back out of the
`forecast_runs` row it just wrote, and computes the decision from the read-back copy.

The store was snapshotted at **2026-09-02T17:15:00Z**, 42,983,424 bytes in, 43,061,248
bytes out, in 0.047 s, and the copy was rebuilt with `Store.rebuild()` over all 36
captures. The snapshot is taken with sqlite3's backup API through a
connection opened `mode=ro` rather than with `cp`, because this session is being recorded
into the source while it runs and a plain file copy leaves the write-ahead log behind.

### Lead time, and where it is not assessable

Design 6.12 scores lead time only over windows whose last context value is below tau.
Three things follow on this cohort and all three are in the tables above.

**At H = 1 a lead can only be 0.** `a*` and `f*` are both `min{h : ... }` over a single
step, so every H = 1 median lead in this experiment is `0.0` or absent, and those cells
are arithmetic rather than information. At H = 4 the medians are 0, 0, 0, 0.5 and 1.0 for
TimesFM-3 and 0, 0, 0, 0 and 1 for `rolling_median`.

**The false-alarm denominators at H = 4 are one or two windows.** Of the five H = 4 runs,
`quiet` is 0 in every one, so a false-alarm rate of 1.00 there is one window out of one.
The number is printed because the rule prints it; it resolves nothing.

**On the pooled rows lead time is "not assessable", and the string is in the file.** tau
is q80 of each capture's own first 32 rows, so the concatenation of twelve captures has
no threshold, and a tau borrowed from one capture scores the other eleven against a level
nobody measured on them. `out/pooled/output_tokens-H1.json` carries
`"lead_time": {"assessable": false, "reason": "tau is q80 of each capture's own first
c_min rows, so the cohort has no single threshold and lead time is not assessable over
pooled windows"}` for all six forecasters, and never a number.

## What it means

**Every one of the nineteen labels is "baseline sufficient", and the branch is taken by
the first inequality in all nineteen.** `E_M > (1 - delta) E_B` holds everywhere: the
best TimesFM-3 run on this cohort is skill 0.0844, and the rule needs skill above 0.10
before the model is allowed past the baselines. The second clause, `W_MB < w`, is
redundant in fourteen of the seventeen per-capture runs and is what a reader should look
at when the first is close: W_MB sits at 0.5128 median at H = 1 and 0.5455 at H = 4,
which is a coin flip window by window.

**No label needed the placebo, and no label could have used it.** "Baseline sufficient"
is reached before the placebo half of the rule is consulted, so W3-T2's absence changed
no verdict here. Had any run been not baseline sufficient, this experiment could only
have written "not baseline sufficient at this n, placebo pending", which is a write-up
standing on one leg. It did not have to: the rule terminated on its first clause every
time.

**Two of the three registry targets are not forecastable on this provider, and that is a
property of Claude Code rather than of any capture.** `fresh_input_tokens` is a constant
0-variation column on all fifteen build sessions, because the prompt cache carries the
context and the fresh input is a handful of tokens per request. `tool_calls_since_prev`
is constant on fourteen of fifteen. No amount of session length fixes a scaled MAD of
exactly 0; the registry is what has to change, and that is an owner decision.

**`output_tokens` is forecastable, and the thing that forecasts it best is a rolling
median of the last eight requests.** Twelve sessions and 1,017 windows at H = 1, five
sessions and 157 windows at H = 4, and `rolling_median` is the best baseline in every
single one. TimesFM-3 is within a few percent of it on mean MAE in both directions and
loses at H = 1 (median skill -0.0225) while winning slightly at H = 4 (median 0.0234).

**The one place the model is clearly different is the shape of its uncertainty, and the
difference is not big enough at this n to carry a claim.** TimesFM-3's calibration flag
is clear in 10 of 12 runs at H = 1 where every baseline's is raised in 12 of 12, its
central-80 coverage sits at 0.7706 median against a nominal 0.80 where `rolling_median`
sits at 0.6688, and its WQS is 0.9759 of the best baseline's at the median of all
seventeen runs. But that ratio crosses 1.0 in five of the seventeen, and at H = 4 the model's own flag is raised
in four of five. Design 6.12's decision rule scores the POINT forecast, and it says
baseline sufficient; the band is a separate question that this experiment did not
pre-register a rule for and therefore does not decide.

**Nothing here needed a longer session; it needed a different question.** The window
check refused 39 of the 73 triples, and every one of those is a capture shorter than 52
requests at H = 1 or 112 at H = 4, exactly the floors W2-T5 measured. The three captures
that failed `output_tokens` at H = 1 have 45, 32 and 5 requests. That part of the
checklist is doing what it was built to do and nothing about it is surprising.

## What we rejected

**That "TimesFM-3 does not beat the baselines" is a statement about TimesFM-3.** It is a
statement about `output_tokens` on the request clock of twelve sessions of one agent on
one machine, scored on the point forecast alone. The model was handed 32 to 512 context
rows of a heavy-tailed count series with ten past-only covariates and no future
covariates at all, and it came within 2 to 3 percent of the best baseline both ways. The
honest reading is that the request clock as compiled today carries little that a rolling
median misses, not that a foundation model is useless on it.

**That the H = 4 result is the interesting one because the skill is positive there.** It
is five runs. The median skill is 0.0234 with a scaled MAD of 0.0064 and a max of 0.0844,
all of them below the 0.10 the rule asks for, and the same five captures are four of the
five longest sessions on the disk. Five is not a sample; it is the number of sessions
this build has run past 112 model requests.

**That the calibration difference is the model being better at uncertainty.** Part of it
is measured to be an artefact of how each band is built: 6.1 percent of `rolling_median`'s
quantile points and 20.5 percent of `local_drift`'s are below zero on a target the
registry declares nonnegative, while `make_positive` from that same registry entry keeps
TimesFM-3's at zero out of 14,805. A band that proposes impossible values has coverage
problems by construction. How much of the flag difference that accounts for is not
measured here, and clipping the baselines to test it changes a pre-registered baseline
definition after seeing results.

**That the pooled row is the headline.** It is printed last and labelled pooled for the
reason design 6.12 gives: a pooled mean over 1,017 windows hides which of twelve sessions
carried it, and the per-capture E_B values run from 549.1 to 1,040.4 tokens. The pooled
rows agree with every per-capture row here, which is worth knowing and is not what makes
the result.

**That E07's own capture should have been excluded from its own cohort.** It is a build
session captured through the launcher exactly like the other fourteen, its request clock
crossed the 52-row floor while the experiment was being written, and dropping it means
choosing the data after seeing which rows exist. It is in the tables at 78 requests
and 46 windows and its numbers sit inside the spread of the other eleven.

## What downstream may now say

A report may say, as a **predictive** claim over the cohort "the request clock of 12
launcher captures of this repository's own build sessions, Claude Code (2.1.258 where
recorded), model opus, content level 1, one machine, 2026-09-02", that at H = 1 over
1,017 rolling-origin windows and at H = 4 over 157, forecasting `output_tokens` with
TimesFM-3 was **baseline sufficient** against the four pre-registered baselines by design
6.12's rule, with `E_M > (1 - delta) E_B` holding in every capture and `W_MB` at 0.5128
median. The claim class is predictive because the forecasts are, and the metrics computed
from observed actuals are derived; neither may be presented as anything stronger, and
the words cause and impact are refused. Every such statement must carry **placebo
pending**: the chronology half of design 6.12's rule needs `block_shuffle` from W3-T2 and
was not run, so nothing here separates "the model learned the order of these rows" from
"the model learned their distribution", and no report may say "temporal evolution" or
"conditional prediction" on the strength of it.

Coverage caveats travel with it. The cohort is one provider and design 6.12 forbids
pooling across providers, so nothing here transfers to Codex. `fresh_input_tokens` and
`tool_calls_since_prev` are **not assessable** on this provider, not "no better than the
baselines": their scaled MAD is 0 and the comparison was never made. Lead time is scored
only inside a capture and is **not assessable** on the pooled rows, because tau is q80 of
each capture's own first 32 rows and the cohort has no single threshold. And at H = 1 a
lead time can only be 0, so the H = 1 lead-time cells are arithmetic rather than
information; the H = 4 cells are the ones that carry any.
