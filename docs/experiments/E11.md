# E11: does knowing a candidate's own diff features change the forecast of what happens after it is merged, on this repository?

**Not assessable, on all three targets, and the reason is the data rather than the
protocol.** The change clock of this repository holds 40 rows in two regimes of 29 and
11, and design 6.12's one-step candidate protocol plans **0 windows** on every one of
`merge_verification_ms`, `merge_verification_failed` and `rework_within_3` against
`k_min` 20. No forecast number, no paired difference and no label exists for this
repository at H = 1. The run reached the STOP its brief names, and the counts are below.

The same runner, the same five forecasters and the same TimesFM-3 checkpoint produce
44 scored windows and a paired difference of 482.887 ms on a synthetic control series
in the same invocation, so "0 windows" is a measurement and not a runner that fell
over.

## Why this matters (30 seconds)

H8 is the hypothesis that the advisory in spec 14.6 rests on: that what anybody can read
off a diff before a merge changes a forecast of what follows the merge. If it holds,
`telltale advise` has something to say at the moment a merge decision is taken. If it
cannot be assessed, every downstream sentence about a candidate is a sentence about a
forecast nobody scored. This run settles which of those two states this repository is
in today, and states exactly what has to change for the question to become answerable.

## Decision rule, written before the run

Quoted verbatim from the task brief, which fixed it before any number was seen (design
6.12 "One-step candidate conditioning" and the readiness checklist):

> Per target in {merge_verification_ms, merge_verification_failed, rework_within_3}:
> (a) readiness fails -> "not assessable", the failing check and its two numbers
> printed; (b) n_windows < k_min -> "not assessable" with n_windows and k_min;
> (c) otherwise the paired median difference in MAE and in pinball loss between
> conditioned and unconditioned, each run's calibration, and the label from `decide` on
> the conditioned run's true order against its placebo; candidate conditioning "changes
> the forecast" iff the paired median MAE difference is negative and its sign holds in
> both halves of the origin range; never "improves prediction" without both halves, and
> never a causal word.

And the STOP the brief pre-registered beside it:

> STOP if fewer than k_min windows exist for every target: write the decision file
> saying so with the counts and what would make it assessable (36 rows inside one regime
> at H = 1, or a rework or a failed merge verification on record), and stop. That is the
> expected outcome on the data of 2026-09-03 and it is a valid result.

Both quotes are verbatim, which is why the second holds one of the three words ADR-014
refuses in forecast output. The word-refusal grep over this file returns that line and no
other; a pre-registered rule quoted exactly is the one place the brief allows it, and the
grep itself is in the VERIFY block of `docs/log/W5-E11.md`.

Two traps were pre-registered with it and neither was sprung: `c_min`, `k_min` and the
origin limit were not lowered to admit windows, and nothing is pooled across
repositories or providers. There is one repository on this disk.

## What it means

**Every target took branch (a), and (b) held underneath it.** Readiness fails first,
because the target's own coverage is not `observed` or `derived`, and the failing check
and its two numbers are `coverage: measured 12, needed 18`. Had coverage passed, clause
(b) refuses next: 0 windows against `k_min` 20. Had the windows existed, check 7 refuses
the two flags: scaled MAD 0.

**The STOP condition of the brief holds exactly.** Fewer than `k_min` 20 windows exist
for every target: 0, 0 and 0. Nothing reached the paired comparison, so this experiment
has no forecast number, no paired median difference, no calibration and no label about
this repository, and no downstream sentence may claim one.

**What makes it assessable.** Every blocker measured, with the number that clears it:

1. **`merge_verification_ms` is `unavailable` on all 40 rows.** No outcome payload in
   this store carries a `duration_ms`; every one predates `telltale outcome
   --duration-ms N`, which W5-T1 added. The merge protocol posting that flag from now on
   is what turns this column from unavailable into observed, one landed change at a
   time.
2. **`merge_verification_failed` is `partial`, one hole, and 0 everywhere else.** The
   hole is row 1, commit `d9b15dc2a270`, whose capture holds no outcome at all. Filling
   it makes the column observed and still leaves the scaled MAD at 0: a failed merge
   verification has to actually occur and be recorded, and the orchestrator merges only
   after a pass, so on this protocol the column is 0 by construction.
3. **`rework_within_3` is `partial` on its last three rows by the rule, and 0 on the
   other 37.** The last three are undecidable until three more changes land, which is
   design 6.12's delayed label and not a defect. Its scaled MAD is 0 for the same reason
   as above: no revert or repair has been posted.
4. **`edit_turnover_ratio` is null on rows 4, 27, 35, 36 and 37.** Row 4 sits inside
   every context this series can form, and no policy imputes, so those five holes cost
   all 13 origins the first regime admits.
5. **The changepoint at row 29 splits 40 rows into 29 and 11.** Twenty origins at H = 1
   and c_min 16 need 36 rows inside one regime. The first regime is closed at 29 and
   cannot grow; the second holds 11, so 25 more landed changes under one environment
   fingerprint is what clears it. The brief's pre-registered "36 rows inside one regime"
   is the same arithmetic and it is confirmed.

The counts have moved since E08, in the right direction and not far enough. E08 measured
this change clock at 23 rows with no changepoint, where the origin formula allowed 7 at
H = 1. It is now 40 rows with one changepoint, the formula allows 24, and the regime that
can carry origins admits 13. The gate is 36 rows in one regime.

## What we rejected

**"The protocol is broken, or the runner is."** The same runner, the same five
forecasters and the same checkpoint scored 44 windows and produced a paired difference,
a calibration table and a decision label on a synthetic series in the same invocation.
Its whole-range numbers reproduce W5-T1's paste and the wave 5 gate's independent re-run
to every digit. A broken runner does not agree with two other environments on
482.88671875.

**"Lower c_min from 16 so shorter contexts are admitted."** The pre-registration
forbids it and the brief named it as a trap, so it was not done. It was measured anyway,
because "forbidden" and "pointless" are different reasons and only one of them is a fact
about this data. On `rework_within_3`, over the same ABLATION_C set, the planner gives 4
windows at c_min 4, 1 at c_min 6, and 0 at c_min 8, 12 and 16 (`out/<target>.json`, key
`c_min_sweep`). The most any value of
c_min buys is 4 against `k_min` 20, because a shorter context still starts at row 0 on
this series and still crosses `edit_turnover_ratio`'s hole at row 4, while a context
short enough to miss row 4 leaves almost no origins behind it. Lowering the floor here
is both forbidden and useless, and it is useless for a reason that is worth writing
down: the binding constraint is where the hole is, not how long the run-up is.

**"Fill `merge_verification_ms` from the wall time of the merge commit."** A duration
recovered from commit timestamps is a different measurement from the duration of a
mechanical verification run, and design invariant 5 is that absence is not zero and a
substitute is not the thing. The column stays unavailable until an outcome payload
carries a real `duration_ms`.

**"Report the 13 origins as the window count."** The candidate protocol runs ABLATION_C
as its past-only set, and over that set the count is 0, not 13. The 13 is the ceiling
the changepoint imposes before the missingness policy is applied. Reported as
n_windows it says the data is one repair away, and it is three.

**"Read the control's 482.887 as something about merging."** It is a difference between
two forecasts of one random walk. It is in this file to show the runner works, and the
mandatory sentence rides on every line of it: the difference between the conditioned and
unconditioned forecast measures how much the candidate's known features change the
forecast; it is not the effect of merging the candidate, because only one future is
observed.

## What downstream may now say

E11 produces **no predictive number and no associative number about this repository**.
The only claim class it establishes is `derived`: the counts, coverage words and window
plans above are derived from observations through `series.build` and the backtester's
planner, and they support exactly one sentence, that the one-step candidate protocol is
not assessable on the change clock of this repository at H = 1 with 0 windows against
`k_min` 20 on all three targets. Any report, advisory or summary that states a forecast
of `merge_verification_ms`, `merge_verification_failed` or `rework_within_3` for this
repository, or a difference between a conditioned and an unconditioned forecast of one,
is stating a number this experiment did not produce. The synthetic control's numbers are
`derived` about a random walk and carry no cohort, so they may be cited only as evidence
that the code runs, never as evidence about a repository, and never pooled with
anything. Coverage caveat, carried forward: `merge_verification_ms` is `unavailable` and
the other two are `partial` on this clock, and "0 failed merge verifications" and "merge
verification failure was not observable on these rows" stay different statements.
Whatever is said next about a candidate, the mandatory sentence of design 6.12 and spec
15.8 rides with it: the difference between the conditioned and unconditioned forecast
measures how much the candidate's known features change the forecast; it is not the
effect of merging the candidate, because only one future is observed.
