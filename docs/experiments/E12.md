# E12: can a reviewer who sees only Telltale's summary of a session diagnose it as well as one who sees the raw stream, and at what cost?

**The full run did not happen.** The pilot ran, both arms, and the raw arm spent 588,848
tokens against a pre-registered stop bound of 400,000. The brief says stop after the
pilot and report when a reviewer exceeds that bound, so this file reports a pilot of two
reviewer sessions and the decision the owner now has. Nothing about the six sessions, the
key, the questionnaire or the rule has moved.

Why this matters (30 seconds): H1 is the claim that Telltale's summary is worth reading.
If a reviewer answers the same diagnostic questions from a 34,649 byte summary as from a
1,440,338 byte stream, the recorder earns its place; if it answers worse, the summary is
a convenience and not evidence. Everything downstream that quotes a summary instead of a
stream rests on this.

## Decision rule, written before the run

Quoted verbatim from `experiments/E12/protocol.md`, section "Pre-registered decision
rule", which was written and committed before any reviewer session existed:

> - Per question and arm: agreement = the number of sessions (of 6) whose answer equals
>   the key exactly. An `unknown` counts as disagreement, and is tallied separately as
>   "declared unknown" because in arm S it is the designed answer wherever Telltale's
>   coverage says partial (a masked exit status, W3-T3).
> - Per question: "summary sufficient" when arm S agrees on at least as many sessions as
>   arm R; "summary insufficient" when arm S agrees on fewer; "neither" when both agree on
>   fewer than 4 of 6. No pooled score across questions is computed: the ten questions
>   are not one thing.
> - Cost: per reviewer session, wall time and total tokens from the launcher's own
>   capture (the reviewer runs under `telltale run --experiment E12`), reported as the
>   median and the full sorted list per arm. H1's claim about cost is a comparison of
>   these two lists, stated with n = 6 per arm and no significance test at that n.
> - What the result can support: a statement about these six sessions, this
>   questionnaire and one reviewer model, claim class comparative. It cannot support a
>   statement about sessions in general or about human reviewers.

And the stop bounds, from the same file, section "Sessions":

> - STOP rules: 10 minutes or 400,000 tokens per reviewer session; stop the arm if the
>   pilot reviewer of that arm answers fewer than 5 of 10 questions.

Those six constants are written into `out/summary.json` under `bounds` by the runner, so
this page quotes them out of the artefact: `stop_tokens` 400000, `stop_wall_ms` 600000,
`stop_min_answers` 5, `sessions_per_arm` 6, `max_turns` 60, `reviewer_model` opus.

The three labels are counts out of six. At one session per arm they are not defined, so
`run.py` prints `not applicable: 1 scored per arm, the rule is written for 6` in the
label column instead of a verdict. That is a refusal, not a result.

## What we did

Environment: one fingerprint for both reviewer sessions,
`env_6242b4f70d81d8bb61e7539af982eadbaa123ad0383309763a4111d2df4f126c`, Claude Code
2.1.259, model `claude-opus-5`, this checkout at `0d3e540`.

1. Built the material for W4-T1/1 in both arms. Arm S is the text of `telltale show`,
   `telltale timeline` and `telltale vector` for `cap_01M1KBDZ8RB9VTF9NRM8TAT3R8` in the
   E12 material store (`TELLTALE_HOME=experiments/E12/out/store`, read-only: no
   `rebuild`, no `outcome`), written as `summary.txt` (4788 bytes), `timeline.txt`
   (26518 bytes) and `vector.txt` (3343 bytes), 34649 bytes together. Arm R is
   `experiments/E12/out/raw/W4-T1-1.jsonl` copied to `session.jsonl`, 1440338 bytes.
2. Put each arm's material in a fresh temporary directory holding nothing else, OUTSIDE
   this repository. Outside is load-bearing twice over: `claude` walks up from its
   working directory looking for a CLAUDE.md, and this checkout has one, with
   `key.json` beside it.
3. Launched one reviewer per arm, in the foreground:

   ```
   telltale run --provider claude --task-id E12-2-<S|R> --attempt 1 --experiment E12 -- \
     claude -p --model opus --permission-mode bypassPermissions --max-turns 60 \
     --output-format stream-json --verbose "<the prompt>"
   ```

   The prompt is built from `key.json`'s own question text, so the questions asked and
   the questions the key answers cannot drift. It is byte-identical in the two arms:
   sha256 `1ff3588bf878e2f6013b50819cc5826451abc75222f83a605796dc1c76a6666f`, recorded in
   both run files. It names no task, no session, no arm and no date, and `run.py` refuses
   to launch if any of the six task ids appears in it.
4. Parsed the last JSON object carrying the ten keys out of the `result` message on the
   child's stdout, scored it against `key.json` by exact match, and wrote
   `out/W4-T1-1-S.json` and `out/W4-T1-1-R.json`. The reviewer's prose was never written
   to disk.
5. Read wall and tokens back from `telltale show` of each reviewer's own capture, never
   from the reviewer's words, and read each capture's file_read paths back to check the
   blinding held.

Wall: 82053 ms for arm S and 105516 ms for arm R, 187569 ms of reviewer wall in total,
inside a harness run of 82690 ms and 106122 ms.

## What it means

**Nothing about H1.** The rule needs six sessions per arm and has one. No question is
labelled, no arm is compared, and the two agreement counts above are a pilot's, not a
result's.

**The stop bound is real and it fired.** Arm R's 588848 tokens is 188848 over the 400000
the owner set. `cache_read_share` 0.907 says where they went: a 1440338 byte file becomes
context, and each of the 15 model requests re-reads it. Arm S spent 149323 across 5
requests on 34649 bytes of material. At the pilot's per-arm medians the full run of 12
sessions projects to 4429026 tokens, against 738171 already spent. That projection is
arithmetic on one session per arm and is an order of magnitude, not a forecast: W3-V/1's
raw file is 1642861 bytes, larger than this one.

**Three things the pilot did settle**, all of which it was written to settle:

1. The raw arm can read a 1440338 byte file inside 60 turns. It used 15, wrote a Python
   script over the stream, and answered all ten questions.
2. Neither arm ran out of questions: 10 of 10 answered in both, so no arm stops on the
   fewer-than-5 rule.
3. Neither arm exceeded the wall bound. 82053 ms and 105516 ms against 600000 ms.

**And one thing it exposed that the protocol predicted and the reviewer walked into.**
protocol.md says arm S is expected to answer `unknown` on Q2 wherever the exit status is
masked. `summary.txt` reports `failed_test_runs: 0` and carries the warning that 11 of 11
verification runs have a masked exit status and that "no test run reported a failing exit
status, which is not the same as every test passing". The reviewer answered 0. It read the
number and not the warning beside it. The key says 6. Q3 and Q5 went the same way: arm S
answered from a summary field, arm R answered from the stream, and the stream was right
each time. Declared unknowns in arm S: 0, where the design expected at least one. Whether
that repeats across six sessions is exactly what the full run would measure.

**Q8 is a question defect, not a key defect.** Both arms answered 2 and the key says 1.
The session made exactly one Bash call whose command contains `git commit`, and that one
call is a shell script containing the string twice. `make_key.py` counts calls
(`sum("git commit" in _command(c) for c in scan.calls)`) and both reviewers counted
occurrences. The key is right and the question's wording lets a careful reader answer 2.
It is frozen and stays frozen: it was written before any reviewer ran.

## What we rejected

**Reading "400,000 tokens" as billable tokens only.** Excluding cache reads gives arm R
54599 and no stop fires. Rejected: a token total in this repository is the sum of the
four counters, E05's stored 173598 for `cap_01M1HQJD1PD1B6QKETVDHWDM6N` is exactly
10 plus 156028 plus 16931 plus 629, and the wave-4 gate set E09's 200000 bound against
that same E05 figure. Choosing the definition that lets the run continue, after seeing
the number, is choosing the answer.

**Scrubbing the task ids out of arm S's timeline.** The VERIFY line for this task expects
0 and the measurement is 2. Rejected: protocol.md defines arm S's material as the text of
three commands, the raw arm is explicitly not scrubbed, and editing one arm's material
after seeing what it contains is a second experiment under a new id. The count is reported
instead.

**Re-running arm R for the two commands that named a path outside the material.**
Rejected: the protocol's invalidation test is the file_read path list, which is empty, and
the two decoded paths are the material directory and the reviewer's own scratch file.
Inventing a stricter test after seeing which arm trips it is the previous paragraph's
error in the other direction. The stricter check stays in the harness and its findings are
printed; it does not invalidate a run.

**Making the reviewer's directory a git repository** so the launcher would print readable
relative paths instead of `<outside>/<8 hex>`. Rejected: the working directory must hold
the material and nothing else, and a `.git` is something else. The digest is computable in
the other direction, so `blinding.py` hashes candidate paths and looks the answer up, and
a digest that decodes to nothing is reported as undecoded rather than assumed harmless.

## What downstream may now say

Nothing about H1, and nothing about the six sessions as a set. The two agreement counts,
6 of 10 and 9 of 10, are one reviewer session each on one session of six, with one
reviewer model, and the pre-registered rule refuses to label at that n. The cost numbers
are usable as cost numbers, claim class **comparative**, cohort "one Opus reviewer, one
session, this questionnaire, 2026-09-03", and they support exactly one statement: on this
session, the raw arm cost 3.943 times the summary arm's tokens and 1.286 times its wall.
No statement about sessions in general, about human reviewers, or about the other five
sessions may be drawn from this file.
