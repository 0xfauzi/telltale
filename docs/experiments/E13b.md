# E13b: does TimesFM-3 clear the baselines on the Codex sessions, the cohort E13 could not build?

Why this matters (30 seconds): E13 answered "baseline sufficient" over 596 runs on Claude
sessions and excluded every Codex capture by measurement: before wave 7 a rollout built a
request clock of 0 rows. W7-T1 gave rollouts a request clock (one model request per
`event_msg/token_count`), and the post-wave-7 census (docs/gates/wave-7.md) counts 781
Codex imports with 32 or more requests, 2,005 ready (capture, target, horizon) triples
and 289,373 windows: four times the Claude population by windows, on a different provider,
a different model family and a surface whose `fresh_input_tokens` varies request to
request (ready on 659 captures at H = 1, against 9 on Claude). If TimesFM-3 is baseline
sufficient here too, H6 is answered on both providers Telltale records.

## Decision rule, written before the run (2026-09-06)

E13's rule, verbatim, on one cohort, `codex/import/level 1`: per (capture, target, H),
E_M = mean MAE of timesfm in true order, E_B = min over the four baselines, W_MB = share
of windows where timesfm beats the best baseline. Baseline sufficient iff
E_M > (1 - delta) E_B or W_MB < w, delta 0.10, w 0.60; otherwise "not baseline sufficient
at this n, placebo pending". Constants: k_min 20, c_min 32, H in {1, 4}, stride H,
baseline window 8, tau q80 of the first 32 rows. Pooled rows per (target, H) over the
cohort are the cohort decision; per-capture labels are reported as counts.

Variant: the registry's `request_past_only`, with the columns readiness check 1 (as
amended by W7-T3) excludes by name because they are `unavailable` on this surface:
`request_duration_ms` (the rollout timestamp rule failed its own bar in W7-T1 and stays
None) and, on captures imported before W7-T2, `env_changed`. The excluded set is recorded
on every run; runs with different excluded sets are not pooled (W7-T3's pooling rule).

Targets: output_tokens, fresh_input_tokens, tool_calls_since_prev, whichever pass
readiness. The census expects tool_calls_since_prev to fail variation on 1,001 of 1,562
triples and output_tokens and fresh_input_tokens to be ready on 659 captures at H = 1 and
343 at H = 4.

What the outcome means: as E13. Baseline sufficient everywhere means more request-clock
data is not the lever on either provider. Any pooled row that is not baseline sufficient
is the number that says a placebo-capable run on this cohort is worth a brief.

Excluded before the run: Claude captures (E13 and E07 scored them), captures with fewer
than 32 model requests (2,095 on the copy), Telltale's own captures.

Runner: experiments/E13b/run.py, E13's mechanics imported by path with the provider
constant flipped; store copy under experiments/E13b/out/home (never ~/.telltale). Pilot
first (`--limit 3`), then the whole cohort on MPS; the pilot's windows per second decide
whether the whole cohort runs in one night or is split.

## What we measured

### Readiness: 1,989 of 4,686 triples are assessable

| target | H = 1 ready | H = 4 ready | H = 1 not | H = 4 not |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| fresh_input_tokens | **655** | **339** | 126 | 442 |
| output_tokens | **655** | **339** | 126 | 442 |
| tool_calls_since_prev | **1** | 0 | 780 | 781 |

First failing check over the 2,697 refusals: `windows` 1,704 (measured 0 to 19 against
20) and `variation` 993 (MAD 0; the check's message prints "needed 0.0" where it means
"more than 0", a wording defect carried to the next fix wave). The census expected
`tool_calls_since_prev` to fail variation on 1,001 of 1,562 triples; it failed on 993 and

every Codex capture ready on `output_tokens` is ready on `fresh_input_tokens` too, because
the rollout's `token_count` varies request to request on both.

inequalities are printed with their values because two of the five rows are decided by
one inequality alone:

| target | H | captures | windows | E_M | E_B (best) | E_M > 0.9 E_B | W_MB | W_MB < 0.6 | label |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| fresh_input_tokens | 1 | 655 | 116,933 | 2,086.6 | 2,425.2 (persistence) | no (2,086.6 vs 2,182.7) | 0.340 | yes | baseline sufficient |
| fresh_input_tokens | 4 | 339 | 25,224 | 2,867.7 | 3,022.0 (rolling_median) | yes | 0.571 | yes | baseline sufficient |
| output_tokens | 1 | 655 | 116,933 | 272.5 | 282.5 (persistence) | yes | 0.341 | yes | baseline sufficient |
| output_tokens | 4 | 339 | 25,224 | 358.2 | 403.4 (rolling_median) | no (358.2 vs 363.1) | 0.591 | yes, by 0.009 | baseline sufficient |
| tool_calls_since_prev | 1 | 1 | 33 | 0.446 | 0.394 (persistence) | yes | 0.212 | yes | baseline sufficient |

Two rows deserve their own sentence. On `fresh_input_tokens` at H = 1 the model's mean MAE
is 14 percent below persistence, yet it beats persistence on only 34 percent of windows:
its errors are smaller where they are large and larger where they are small, which is a
distribution the mean hides and the win share shows, and it is the case the rule's second
inequality was written for. On `output_tokens` at H = 4 both numbers lean the model's
way and the win share misses w by 0.009; 71 of its 339 per-capture runs (21 percent)
could not be called baseline sufficient, against 9 of 174 (5 percent) on the Claude
imports in E13.

The 108 runs the rule could not call baseline sufficient: 6 on `fresh_input_tokens` H = 1,
26 at H = 4, 5 on `output_tokens` H = 1, 71 at H = 4. The full list with windows, E_M / E_B,
W_MB and skill is in `summary.json` under `runs` where `decision.label` is not
`baseline sufficient`. The largest: `imp_e5a8efe5ff59feb34a641754`, `output_tokens`,
skill 0.352 over 1,070 windows at H = 1 and 0.363 over 267 windows at H = 4, the one
capture where the model beats the best baseline by a third at both horizons.

## What it means

Every pooled row took the "baseline sufficient" branch of the rule written before the run,
so the rule's consequence stands on both providers: more request-clock data is not the
lever, and no placebo brief is triggered by this cohort. That is the finding, and the
margin on one row is part of it: `output_tokens` at H = 4 is baseline sufficient because
the model won 59.1 percent of windows and the rule asks for 60.

The constants were fixed on 2026-09-06 before any Codex window was scored and are not
moved here. A placebo-capable rerun of the `output_tokens` H = 4 cohort under the same
constants would be a new experiment id with its own pre-registration, and it would cost
about 25,224 windows times six orderings (true plus five seeds), about 7.3 hours at the
measured 5.74 windows per second. It is proposed, not run.

## What we rejected

- **Calling the H = 4 output row "not baseline sufficient" because it is close.** The rule
  is `W_MB < w` and 0.591 is below 0.6. A rule read as "unless it is close" is not a rule.
- **Pooling across providers.** E13's Claude rows and these Codex rows are different
  surfaces, models and tokenizers; the pre-registration forbids the pool and it is not made.
- **Reading the 108 per-capture "pending" labels as evidence of a subpopulation.** They are
  reported as counts, as the rule says. 71 of 339 at one (target, H) is the number a
  follow-up would be registered on, not a claim.
- **Lowering k_min or c_min to admit the 2,697 refused triples.** Every refusal names its
  measured number against the constant, and the constants stay.

## How sure we are

- 1,989 runs, 284,347 windows, every one scored by the same backtester as E13 on the same
  rule: measured.
- The reading of the `fresh_input_tokens` H = 1 row (lower mean error, lower win share) as a
  heavy-tail effect: reasoned from the two numbers, not measured per window.
- That the 4 captures missing against the census at each horizon change nothing: judgement,
  they are 0.6 percent of the ready set.
- Why 21 percent of `output_tokens` H = 4 runs are not baseline sufficient on Codex against
  5 percent on Claude: unknown.

## What downstream may now say

On the cohort `codex/import/level 1` (781 imported Codex sessions with 32 or more model
requests, content level 1, variant `request_past_only` with `request_duration_ms` and
`env_changed` excluded by name), TimesFM-3 is baseline sufficient for `fresh_input_tokens`,
`output_tokens` and `tool_calls_since_prev` at H = 1 and H = 4 by the pre-registered rule,
claim class `predictive`, coverage as recorded on each run. Reports may say that the win
share on `output_tokens` at H = 4 was 0.591 against a threshold of 0.6, and that 108 of
1,989 per-capture runs were not baseline sufficient at their n. Nothing here is a cause and
nothing here says what a session would do.
