## WHAT WAS WRONG

Nothing in wave 1 or wave 2 was wrong. Four things were undecided, and each is decided
here with the evidence beside it.

1. **The checklist's window count had to be the backtester's, and the backtester's plan
   was not reachable.** `_plan`, `_regime_start` and `_tau` were private, and
   `run()` was the only way to reach any of them, which would have meant running four
   forecasters over every window to count the windows. Rather than copy the origin
   arithmetic (the brief's STOP), backtest.py gained four public functions:
   `plan(series, target, horizon, *, c_min=None) -> Plan`, `registered(series, target,
   horizon) -> TargetSpec` (the three refusals `run` already made, factored out and
   called by both), `regime_start(changepoints, origin)` and `threshold(series, target,
   c_min=None)`. `_Plan` became `Plan` and gained a `columns` field, so the checklist
   reads the frame the backtester selected rather than reselecting it. `run()` calls
   the same code and its behaviour is unchanged: the full suite passed before and after
   that commit.

2. **Design 6.12's window formula disagrees with the plan, and by a lot.** The formula
   `floor((N - H - c_min) / s) + 1` knows nothing about changepoints. On the 200-row
   synthetic series at H = 1 it says 168 and the plan runs 136; the 32 missing origins
   are 120 to 151, each of them fewer than c_min rows after the changepoint at 120. So
   the check compares k_min against the plan, and prints the formula beside it. On a
   7-row series the formula says -25, which is printed verbatim: clamping it to 0 would
   hide that the formula does not know N can be smaller than c_min.

3. **Check 3 has to count the cells the backtester reads, not the cells inside a
   rectangle.** A window is `rows[ctx_start:o]` over the frame columns PLUS
   `target[o:o + H]`, and the actual block reads the target column alone (`_block` in
   backtest.py is called with `indices[:1]`). My first version counted every frame
   column over `[ctx_start, o + H)`, and the test caught it: a None in
   `cache_read_tokens` at row 60 was reported as sitting in the window at origin 60,
   which does not read that cell. The window that does read it is 61, the first origin
   whose context covers row 60. Counting the wider rectangle would fail a series for
   cells no forecaster ever sees.

4. **Two checks can be vacuously true, and a vacuous pass reads like a pass.** Checks 4
   and 6 say "n_ctx >= 9 at every origin" and "n_ctx >= 2B at every origin". Over zero
   origins both are true and both are empty. They report `measured -` and FAIL, with
   the words "no window was retained, so no context was measured". That is a judgement
   and someone could reasonably differ: the alternative is a real capture whose
   checklist shows six passes and two failures when nothing was measured at all.

## UNSURE OR NOT DONE

- **Check 1 fails on every replayed capture, and that is a decision.** Design 6.12 says
  "target and variant columns observed or derived-from-observed". I read "variant
  columns" as the columns the pre-registered variant NAMES, which for
  `request_past_only` is every other column of the series. So Claude S1 fails at 9 of
  10 (`env_changed` is unavailable: no launcher stamped a fingerprint onto a replayed
  fixture) and Codex S1 at 8 of 10. The backtester's own behaviour is different and
  unchanged: it drops such a column from the variant and warns. The narrower reading,
  where "variant columns" means only the columns that survived selection, would make
  check 1 a test of the target alone and would pass every capture on this disk. I chose
  the reading that says out loud that the run would not be the pre-registered variant.
  A launcher capture passes check 1 (the build capture above scores 10 of 10).
- **Checks 4 and 6 read the same number.** Both are the shortest retained context, and
  with c_min at 32 they cannot fail while check 2 passes: an origin the plan retains
  has n_ctx >= c_min = 32, which is above 9 and above 8. They are two lines because the
  design lists two, and they would separate under a smaller c_min. Neither has been
  seen to fail for its own reason.
- **Check 7's "retained rows" is every row of the series.** Under policy `exclude` no
  row is dropped at build time; windows are dropped. So the scaled MAD is over every
  known target value. A series whose variation sits entirely outside every planned
  window would pass check 7 and be flat everywhere a forecaster looks. That case needs
  a changepoint late in a long series and I have not constructed one.
- **The summary field is computed at `show` time, not stored.** `summary()` builds the
  request-clock series on every call, which for the 32-row build capture is
  imperceptible and for a 5000-row capture has not been measured. It is a pure function
  of the stored activities, so two `show` calls agree; no Evidence row is written and
  nothing about rebuild changes.
- **No absolute threshold exists to test check 8's other branch.** All three registry
  entries carry `threshold_rule = "q80_first_c_min"`, so the `declared` branch of
  `_threshold` is written and never taken. The detail line says so on every run.
- **The horizon in the summary field is 1 and only 1.** A capture ready at H = 1 and
  not at H = 4 shows as ready. The field carries `"horizon": 1` beside the entries so
  the claim is bounded, and `forecast readiness --horizon 4` is the way to ask the
  other question.
- **Nothing here has run against a target whose own coverage is partial.** That case
  raises `backtest.Refused` out of `check()` before any line is produced, and
  `summary_field` catches it into `{"refused": ...}` per target. No capture on this
  disk reaches it: all three targets ride the `request_usage` capability, which is
  observed on Claude and on Codex.

# EXPLAIN FOR THE OWNER

## The failure this exists to prevent

A forecast that should never have been made does not look wrong. It comes back with
numbers of the usual size, in the usual units, with a spread around them, and a table
comparing it against four baselines. Nothing on the page says that the series behind it
was seven rows long, or that the column being forecast was the same number every time,
or that every window the score is built from was dropped and the score is over nothing.

So the question is not "was the forecast good". It is "was there anything here to
forecast at all", and it has to be asked first, because after the numbers exist
somebody will read them.

That is what this task built: a preflight. Eight questions, asked of a series before
any model touches it, each answered with two numbers and a plain sentence.

## What a window is, and why counting them is the whole job

Start with how a forecaster is scored on a session that has already happened.

Pick a row in the middle of the series. Call it the origin. Hand the forecaster the
rows before it and nothing else, ask it for the next row, and compare its answer
against the row you did not show it. Then move the origin forward and do it again.
Each of those is one window, and the score is the average error over all of them.

Two rules bound which origins are usable. A window needs a minimum amount of history
behind it, 32 rows on the request clock, which is the pre-registered number `c_min`. And
a run needs a minimum number of windows before its average means anything, 20, which is
`k_min`. Both were fixed in the design before any data was seen, which is what makes
them decision rules rather than results.

So the second check on the list is: how many windows will this series actually produce.

## The number the design gives you is the wrong number

Design 6.12 states the count as a formula: rows, minus the horizon, minus c_min,
divided by the stride, plus one. On the 200-row synthetic series that is 168.

The backtester runs 136.

The difference is the changepoint. A changepoint is a row where the environment
fingerprint differs from the row before it: a model switch, a version bump, anything
that makes the rows after it not the same kind of thing as the rows before it. The
backtester will not read across one. It starts a window's history at the last
changepoint, and if that leaves fewer than 32 rows of history it skips the origin
entirely rather than padding it from the previous regime.

The synthetic series has its changepoint at row 120. Origins 120 through 151 have
fewer than 32 rows since it. Thirty-two origins, gone, and the formula cannot see them
because the formula does not know changepoints exist.

This is why the checklist does not implement the formula. It calls the backtester's own
planning function and counts what comes back. Both numbers are printed, because the
gap between them is a fact about the series:

```
windows  pass  136  20  200 rows, c_min 32, H 1, stride 1: formula 168, planned 136; dropped: regime_too_short 32
```

Making that possible needed a change in the backtester. Its planner was private and the
only way to reach it was to run a full backtest, four baselines over every window,
which is an expensive way to count. So it now exposes the planner directly, and the
checklist calls the same code the backtest calls. A second copy of that arithmetic
would have been a checklist for a backtest nobody runs.

## The eight lines, on two real series

Left, the synthetic series: 200 rows of a seeded random walk, written by
`tests/integration/synthetic_series.py`, with one changepoint at row 120. Right, this
build's own longest capture: 32 model requests, a real session, read from a copy of the
recorder's database.

| check | what it asks | synthetic, 200 rows | the build capture, 32 requests |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| coverage | was every column actually seen | pass, 10 of 10 | pass, 10 of 10 |
| windows | enough origins after the exclusions | pass, 136 against 20 | FAIL, 0 against 20 |
| missingness | any unknown inside a window | pass, 0 of 1982 cells | pass, 0 of 0 cells |
| baselines | enough history for local drift | pass, 32 against 9 | FAIL, nothing measured |
| changepoints | one regime long enough to use | pass, 120 against 33 | FAIL, 32 against 33 |
| placebo | enough history to shuffle | pass, 32 against 4 | FAIL, nothing measured |
| variation | does the target move at all | pass, 959.24 | FAIL, exactly 0 |
| threshold | is the alarm level computable | pass, tau 1606.4 | pass, tau 2 |

Read the right-hand column downwards and the session tells its own story. Thirty-two
requests is exactly c_min, so the first usable origin is one row past the end of the
series: zero windows. With no windows there is no context to measure, so the two
history checks have nothing to report and say so rather than passing on an empty set.
The one regime is 32 rows where 33 are needed. And the target does not move: this
capture spent 64 fresh input tokens over 32 requests, which is 2 every time, so the
spread of the column is exactly zero. Every baseline would score a perfect zero error
on it, and skill, which divides by the best baseline's error, would have no
denominator.

The last line is the interesting one. tau, the level at which a forecast counts as an
alarm, is computable: it is the 80th percentile of the first 32 rows, and on a column
of 2s that is 2. Computable and useless are different things, and the checklist reports
the first honestly while the variation line reports the second.

## What the two missingness policies do to one gap

A missing value in this system is `None`, never zero, because "no verification has run
yet" and "the verification returned 0" are different statements and only one of them is
a number.

The request clock has a column called `last_verification_exit`: the result of the most
recent test run, as of that request. On the first row of essentially every session it
is unknown, because the agent makes a request before it runs a test. The column is
marked observed, since this surface can see verification results. So there is a real
hole in a column that was really observable.

Two policies, both refusing to invent anything, differing in when they stop.

`refuse` stops at build time:

```
$ uv run telltale series build --clock request --capture capS1 --policy refuse
policy refuse: column last_verification_exit has coverage observed and no value in row 0 (activity act_6cd3d2810ec5c1ae1287bdcaca)
$ echo $?
2
```

Column, row, and the activity the row was built from. No series is written.

`exclude`, the default, builds the series with the `None` still in it and lets the
backtester drop the windows that would have to read it. That is what the third check
counts, and it is where the interesting question lives: which cells count as inside a
window.

Not a rectangle. A window reads every column over its history rows, but only the target
column over the rows it is being scored on. My first version counted the whole
rectangle, and the test caught it: a hole in `cache_read_tokens` at row 60 was reported
as sitting in the window at origin 60, and the window at origin 60 never reads that
cell. The first window that does read it is the one at origin 61, whose history covers
row 60. On the 200-row series that one hole drops 59 windows, all of them for the same
reason, and the backtester names the reason itself:

```
missing_context_value(cache_read_tokens)   59
```

The check now names the same window the backtester drops, because it counts the same
cells.

And the mirror case: a hole nobody reads is not a failure. Move the changepoint to row
10 and every surviving window starts there, so a hole at row 5 is read by nothing. The
series is ready with an unknown still in it. That is precisely what `exclude` means,
and a check that failed on it would refuse a forecast for nothing.

## How long a session has to be

The checklist is now in the session summary, one entry per forecastable target, so
`telltale show` on any capture says whether that capture can be forecast and, if not,
which line failed with which numbers. It replaces a boolean that said only "this
session made at least one model request", which was true of every capture and told a
reader nothing.

On this disk, every capture answers no. So the obvious question is what would answer
yes, and it is measurable rather than arguable. Synthetic series at increasing lengths,
no changepoint and no gaps, which is the most generous case a real session could be:

```
51 rows   19 windows   NOT ready
52 rows   20 windows   ready
```

Fifty-two model requests, forecasting one step ahead. At four steps ahead the origins
step by four, so the same 20 windows need 112 requests. Both numbers assume the session
never changes environment mid-flight; one changepoint in the middle costs about 32
origins, as the synthetic series shows.

The longest capture recorded so far is 32 requests. So nothing in the forecasting
laboratory can be exercised on real data yet, and the checklist is what makes that a
stated fact on the face of every summary rather than something a reader would have to
work out from a table of zeros.
