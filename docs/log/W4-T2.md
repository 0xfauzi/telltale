### The public signatures

```python
# src/telltale/profile.py
GROUPINGS = ("week", "subsystem", "path")
BACKFILL = " (backfill)"          # what a group of imported captures is called
EXCLUDED / NO_PATHS / NO_EDIT / NOTHING_UNDER   # why a capture is in no group
class Refused(ValueError)          # unknown repo_id, or --by path with no --path

build(store, repo_id, path=None, by=None, include_backfill=False) -> dict

# src/telltale/report_profile.py
REFUSED_WORDS = ("maintainability", "quality", "difficulty", "score")
class ForbiddenWord(Exception)
refuse_words(text) -> str
render(built) -> str
command(args) -> int               # cli.py dispatches "profile" here
add_commands(subcommands) -> None  # cli.py registers the subcommand through this
```

`build` returns

```
{repo_id, by, path, include_backfill, captures, grouped, skipped: {reason: count},
 groups: [{group, composition, cohort, rows: [row, ...22]}]}
```

and a row is `asdict(Evidence.comparative)` (the group's median as `value`) plus `family`,
`n`, `unknown`, `mad_scaled`, `min`, `max` and `ratio`. The ratio cell is either a second
comparative Evidence as a dict, with `cohort_median` and `n_metric`, or
`{"no_ratio": <sentence>}` naming what stopped it. It is never blank, never 1.0 and never
0.

## WHAT WAS WRONG

Nothing was broken. Three things had to be worked out before any of it could be written,
and two of them changed the design.

**Design 6.11's cohort gate does not transfer.** The existing rule ranks ONE capture
against a cohort of at least ten captures sharing four keys, and every member of that
cohort is a peer of the capture being ranked. A work profile ranks a GROUP of captures,
and the group is usually inside the cohort. Measured on a `.backup` copy of the owner's
store on 2026-09-03: this repository's five subsystem groups are 16, 7, 1, 19 and 12
captures, drawn from a single cohort of 31. `src` is 19 of the 31, so a "cohort median"
that included it would be mostly `src`'s own median, and the ratio would sit near 1.0 by
construction, most strongly for the biggest group. The gate and the denominator both move
to the cohort MINUS the group. Amendment, 6.11.

**A group has to be one cohort before it can be placed against one.** Spec 16's allowed
sentence ends "same model/runtime family", and a group whose captures do not all share
the four keys has no such family. Measured: the DEFAULT profile of this repository is one
ISO week holding 43 captures, of which 31 are build sessions (claude, runtime major 2,
opus, content level 1), 10 are `telltale run -- true` smoke captures whose provider is
`generic` and whose runtime and model nothing states, and 2 have no provider at all. The
whole week is refused, with "12 of 43 captures have an unknown cohort key". That is not
a gap: a distribution over a smoke test and a build session mixed together is not a
cohort-qualified statement about anything, and `--by subsystem` fills the column.
Amendment, 6.11.

**The scripted agent could not touch a subsystem.** `tests/integration/fake_agent.py`
rewrites one file, `answer.txt`, at the repository root, so every capture of it lands in
one group and the subsystem grouping had nothing to test. The first fix I tried was to
launch it from a subdirectory. Measured, and it does not work: running the launcher with
`cwd=repo/src` records the Edit's `file_path` as `answer.txt`, because the path on a tool
call is the one the agent wrote and nothing rewrites a relative path against the
repository root. The flag is the fix. Amendment, test fixture.

## WHAT CHANGED

### `src/telltale/profile.py`: the aggregation

`build` does five things in order.

1. **Resolve the grouping.** `week` by default. `--path PREFIX` selects the captures that
   edited under the prefix and, when `--by` is absent, also NAMES the one group they
   form: spec 16 says "for each path/subsystem", and its allowed sentence is about one
   prefix. `--by path` with no prefix is refused rather than defaulted.
2. **Find the captures and their cohort keys.** `store.captures()` filtered by `repo_id`,
   oldest first; `cohorts.cohort_keys` per capture. A `repo_id` nobody stored is a
   refusal naming how many repositories are stored and the busiest five, not an empty
   profile: "this repository has no captures" and "that is not a repo_id" are different
   answers.
3. **Place each capture.** Imported captures are skipped unless `--include-backfill`, and
   then grouped on their own with a ` (backfill)` suffix, because design 6.11 puts them
   outside every cohort and mixing one into a group would take the whole group's cohort
   away. A capture belongs to EVERY subsystem it edited, so subsystem groups overlap. A
   capture that lands nowhere is counted under the sentence that says why: it edited no
   file, it edited nothing under the prefix, or it made edits whose paths no surface
   named. `grouped` against `captures` in the frame is what makes those visible.
4. **Build the rows.** One `Evidence.comparative` per (group, metric), value = the
   group's median, source = the evidence ids it was taken over, coverage = the weakest
   among them. `experiments_measure.stats` supplies n, median, scaled MAD, min and max,
   so this repository has one spelling of "scaled MAD" and not two.
5. **Build the ratio.** The group's median over the median of the cohort outside the
   group, when at least ten members are outside and at least ten of them measured that
   metric, and the cohort median is not 0.

Reads are memoized because a capture is in more than one subsystem group and is usually
in the cohort those groups are placed against as well. That took the subsystem profile of
this repository from 1.98 s to 1.03 s.

### `src/telltale/report_profile.py`: the renderer, the command, and the refusal

The frame, one sample-composition table, 22 metric tables (one per metric, rows are the
groups, so the comparison a reader wants is a column), and a note. `render_table` is
imported from report.py rather than copied, so a profile table and a vector table are the
same table.

`refuse_words` checks the FINISHED string against `maintainability`, `quality`,
`difficulty` and `score`, before anything is printed, the way `forecast.refuse_words`
checks `cause`, `impact` and `would` under ADR-014. It reads the data as well as the
headings, and the cost of that is stated rather than hidden: a repository with a
top-level directory named `quality` cannot be profiled by subsystem, and the refusal says
which word and that the profile may be taken by week instead. A check over the headings
alone would pass a table whose group column carried the word, and a per-path table is
exactly the shape somebody would add a latent scalar to.

The one `print` outside `cli*.py` and `report.py` carries a targeted `# noqa: T201` and
the reason: the string it prints is the one `refuse_words` has just checked, and a caller
that received the text and printed it elsewhere could print an unchecked one just as
easily.

### `src/telltale/cli.py`: three lines

`report_profile` in the import tuple, `report_profile.add_commands(subcommands)` beside
the two calls already there, and `"profile": report_profile.command` in `_COMMANDS`.
Nothing else, including no paragraph in the module docstring, because the brief asked for
the smallest possible hunk while W4-T1 was in flight.

### `tests/integration/fake_agent.py`: `--target PATH`

Defaults to `answer.txt`, so every existing caller is byte-identical. `_act` now writes
the path the Edit call names instead of the module constant, which is the same file
unless `--target` moved it.

## UNSURE OR NOT DONE

- **The default grouping is degenerate on this repository, and will stop being so.**
  Every capture of this repository is in ISO week 2026-W36, so `--by week` is one group
  today. The command is right and the data is young. Nothing to fix; stated so that a
  reader of the first output is not surprised.
- **`briefs` is a group only with `--include-backfill`.** The brief predicted `src,
  tests, docs, briefs, experiments`. Measured: this repository has exactly two `file_edit`
  rows under `briefs/`, `briefs/README.md` and `briefs/W1-T4.md`, and both belong to
  imported captures rather than to launcher ones. So the default profile has no `briefs`
  group and `--include-backfill` prints `briefs (backfill)` with 2 captures and no
  cohort. The orchestrator writes the briefs, and the orchestrator's own sessions reach
  this store through the transcript importer.

- **A subsystem group can be `<outside>`, which is not a directory.**
  `sanitize.relativize` rewrites a path outside the repository as `<outside>/<8 hex>`,
  one token per directory, so a capture that edited outside the tree lands in a group
  called `<outside>`. One imported capture does, on the owner's store. Dropping it would
  report work that happened as work that did not; the token is the sanitizer's own
  spelling and says on the row that it is not part of this repository. This is stated in
  `profile._names` and is a judgement call somebody could differ on.
- **The ratio's denominator excludes the group, and the brief's example number did not.**
  The brief's expected cell was `no cohort (n=3)` for a three-capture cohort. The cell
  now reads `no cohort (n=3, 0 outside this group)`, because the gate is the outside
  count and printing the cohort size alone would say "n=3" beside a rule that reads
  "at least ten". Both numbers are printed; the amendment states the rule.
- **A group of one gets a ratio.** `fixtures` is one capture and its row reads `1.643x`.
  There is no lower bound on the GROUP's size, only on the cohort outside it, and the
  brief set none. `n` is in the row and `mad_scaled` is `-`, so the reader can see it is
  one capture. Whether a floor belongs there is a decision, not a measurement, and it is
  the owner's.
- **The word refusal can refuse a real repository.** A top-level directory named
  `quality`, `score`, `difficulty` or `maintainability` makes `--by subsystem` unprintable
  for that repository. The refusal names the word and says to profile by week. This is a
  deliberate trade and it is written in report_profile.py's first paragraph.
- **`--by subsystem` and `--path` both read `file_edit` activities and not the linked
  commit's `per_file` list.** W3-T4 put paths on `telltale.repo.commit`, and 4 of 19
  change rows on the owner's store carry one. `file_edit` covers 29 of this repository's
  49 captures and needs no commit link, so it is the wider surface; a capture with an
  edit and no linked commit still lands in a group. The two could disagree, and this
  command does not reconcile them.

## EXPLAIN FOR THE OWNER

### The question this command answers, and the one it refuses

You want to know whether some part of your repository is harder to work in than another.
The honest form of that question is: over the sessions that touched this directory, what
did the work look like, and how does it compare with sessions like it that touched
something else?

The dishonest form is a single number per directory. Call it 0.17 and it sounds like a
measurement; it is a weighted sum whose weights nobody chose in public. Your
specification already refuses it twice, in section 16 and in ADR-008, and it says what is
allowed instead: a comparison, against a named group of comparable sessions, with the
size of that group printed beside it.

So this command prints distributions and one comparison, and never a total.

### What a "cohort" is, and why the profile keeps hitting it

Two sessions are comparable if they ran on the same provider, the same major version of
that provider's runtime, the same model and the same content level. That set of
comparable sessions is what the code calls a cohort, and it has to hold at least ten
captures before any rank or ratio is printed. Below ten, a rank is mostly a statement
about which nine other sessions happen to be in your database.

That rule already existed for comparing one capture against the rest. This task found
that it does not transfer to comparing a GROUP of captures. On your store, the sessions
that edited under `src/` are 19 of the 31 captures in their cohort. If the "cohort
median" included them, most of what `src` was being compared against would be `src`. So
the comparison is against the cohort MINUS the group, and the gate counts that outside
set.

### One real row, with real values

From your own store, on 3 September 2026, `telltale profile <this repo> --by subsystem`:

```
context_token_burden / fresh_input_tokens
GROUP  N   UNKNOWN  MEDIAN  MAD_SCALED  MIN  MAX  UNIT    COVERAGE  CLAIM_CLASS  RATIO_TO_COHORT_MEDIAN
docs   16  0        237.0   109.7124    64   378  tokens  observed  comparative  1.197x (comparative, n=15)
```

Read it as a sentence: sixteen sessions of this repository edited a file under `docs/`.
All sixteen reported a fresh input token count, so nothing is missing. Their median was
237 tokens. They used 1.197 times the median of the fifteen comparable sessions that did
not touch `docs/`, where comparable means claude, runtime major version 2, model opus,
content level 1, a cohort of 31 of which 15 sit outside this group.

Every number there was measured. The 237 and the 1.197 come from the evidence rows the
reducer wrote at the end of each capture, read back rather than recomputed, so this row
and `telltale show` of any one of those sixteen captures cannot disagree. The 16, the 15
and the 31 are counts of captures in your database at the moment the command ran.

### Why "1.197x the cohort median" is allowed and "docs is 0.17" is not

Three differences, and each one is a thing a reader can check.

The first is that the ratio names its comparison set. "1.197 times the median of fifteen
sessions that share these four properties" can be wrong, and you can find out by looking
at the fifteen. A single number per directory names nothing, so there is nothing to
check.

The second is that the ratio is one metric. `docs` is 1.197 times the cohort on fresh
input tokens and 1.384 times on model requests, and those are two facts. A single number
would have to combine them, and the combining weights would be an opinion wearing the
costume of a measurement.

The third is that the ratio is honest about what it does not know. These sessions were
not assigned to `docs/`; they arrived there. They differ from the other fifteen in the
task they were given, in what the repository looked like that day, and in a dozen things
nobody recorded. The ratio says "these sessions used more", which is true. It does not
say "docs makes sessions use more", which would need an experiment. That is why every one
of these numbers carries the word `comparative` in its own column, and why the code
refuses to build them as anything stronger.

The word check is the last line of that defence. The renderer scans the finished text for
`maintainability`, `quality`, `difficulty` and `score` and raises rather than printing.
It is a blunt instrument, and it costs something real: a repository with a top-level
directory called `quality` cannot be profiled by subsystem, and the error says so and
tells you to profile by week. I chose that over a check that reads only the headings,
because a check that skipped the data would pass a table whose group column carried the
very word.

### What the default view told me, and why I left it alone

The first thing I ran was `telltale profile` with no flags, on your store. It printed one
group, `2026-W36`, and refused the ratio column on all 22 metrics with "12 of 43 captures
have an unknown cohort key".

That looked like a failure and is not. Two things are true at once. Every capture of this
repository so far is inside one ISO week, so there is nothing yet for a week grouping to
separate. And the 43 captures are not all the same kind of thing: 31 are build sessions,
10 are `telltale run -- true` smoke captures with no model and no runtime version, and 2
have no provider recorded. A median over a smoke test and a four-hour build session
mixed together is not a cohort-qualified statement about anything, so the command says so
instead of printing a number.

I could have made the profile quietly drop the captures that spoil the cohort. That is
the change I rejected. It would have printed a filled column today at the price of a
number whose sample nobody could see. The composition table is the alternative: it shows
you `providers: claude 31, generic 10, unknown 2` and lets you decide.
