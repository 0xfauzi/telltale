## WHAT WAS WRONG

Design 6.12 spells the change clock's process columns "summed over the attempts that
landed it", and `series_changes._change_row` read that literally: `landed` was the
activities of `change.landed_by`, the attempts among the captures that recorded the
commit, and every one of the seven columns and the row's environment fingerprint came off
that list. A capture that recorded a commit and never said which attempt of which task it
was reached the row only as a `repo_commit` activity: it supplied the six repository
columns and the rung, and its tokens, compactions, verification runs, edits and reads sat
in the store unread.

That was a reasonable reading while the launcher was the only way a session was recorded.
It stopped being one on 2026-09-06, when the owner switched on day-to-day capture. W9-T1
gave such a session its repository, so its commits reach the clock; its row printed six
repository columns `observed` and seven process columns `unavailable`, and the cohort's
dropped list called the capture

> no task_id and attempt: not an attempt of this lineage

which is true of the attempt clock and misleading here, because the capture had just
filled a row of this one.

## WHAT CHANGED

**`series_changes.Recorder`** is new: one capture that recorded a commit at a ladder rung
and named no attempt. It carries the five fields an `Attempt` carries about the capture
behind it, read off the same lifecycle row (`series_lineage.capture_row`), and none of the
four an attempt identity supplies.

**`Change.recorded_by`** holds those captures, in start order, for the commit or its tree
twin. **`Change.process_from`** is `landed_by or recorded_by`, and it is what
`_change_row` folds the seven process columns over, what `Change.fingerprint` reads, what
`Change.activities` names in the row's provenance, and what the frame's coverage is folded
over. A git-history import is never a recorder: it writes `unlinked`, which is not a rung.

**`attempts_to_land`** is unchanged: the maximum attempt ordinal among the attempts that
landed the change, and None when none did.

**The four post-merge columns** keep the attempts as their input, with a comment at the
call saying why: an outcome reaches a change through the task id and attempt ordinal it
names, and a recorder that stated neither cannot be found by one.

**The cohort** gained `process_from_recording_captures`, the row keys whose process
columns came from a recorder, in row order and cut to the retained rows. A capture whose
columns were folded is dropped with a sentence that says so instead of the one above:

> recorded &lt;sha&gt; at &lt;rung&gt;: process columns folded, attempts_to_land unknown

A capture that recorded a commit at a rung and has no capture lifecycle activity is
refused by name (`series_changes.NO_COVERAGE`) and folded into nothing: no coverage word
is defaulted for it. Measured on the E16 store copy, that refusal has never fired: all 33
captures that recorded a commit at a rung have the row, as do all 1459 codex captures.

**The test** is `tests/integration/test_series_recorded.py`, a new file rather than an
extension of `test_series_lineage.py` as the brief asked: written there it took that file
from 588 lines to 851 and the 800-line ratchet refused the commit. The split is the one
W8-T2 already made under the same pressure, and the new file imports the same helpers
`test_series_backfill.py` does. It builds two daemon captures over one repository through
a real receiver, one carrying a correlation and one carrying nothing, and asserts that
each row's tokens are its own session's, that the attempt's row carries its ordinal and
the recorder's carries None, that `env_changed` is None, that the cohort names the sha
and that `series check` still resolves the recorder's activities against the row's end.
Run against this branch's parent it fails on the second row's
`fresh_input_tokens_total`, which is None there and 17 here.

`series_lineage.py` changed in three small ways and no others: `_capture_row` and
`_READ_TYPES` are now public, because the change clock reads a recording capture's five
fields off the same row and folds the same activity slice, and the sentence above is now
the constant `NOT_AN_ATTEMPT` so that the two files cannot drift about it. The file is 799
lines against the 800 ratchet.

### The one thing the fold costs, measured

Frame coverage is folded over whatever each row folded, which is DO (2) of the brief and
is what stops a column reading `observed` while a capture that could not see it filled
rows. It has a consequence the brief did not anticipate, and
`tests/integration/test_series_backfill.py` caught it on the first full run:
`series_lineage.worst_coverage` collapses disagreement to the WEAKEST word, so one
recorder whose capability map is `unavailable` everywhere takes the six capability-backed
process columns of the WHOLE frame to `unavailable`, and `series.blank_unobservable` then
empties them, including on a row an attempt landed.

Measured on that fixture: the twin row held `fresh_input_tokens_total` 786,
`cache_read_tokens_total` 6000, `compactions` 0, `verification_cycles` 0,
`edit_turnover_ratio` 1.0 and `unique_files_read` 1 before this task, and holds None in
all six after, because the same frame holds a synthetic session capture that delivered no
surface and recorded two commits at rungs. `stable_state_intervals` keeps its 1: it rests
on no capability, so its word comes from its own cells. That test's assertion is updated
and the comment there names this task and the reason.

It is the safe direction and not merely the briefed one. The recorder's own cells for
those columns include counts (`compactions`, `verification_cycles`, `unique_files_read`)
that are 0 by arithmetic over activities a capture with no surfaces could never have had,
and `unavailable` is what stops a 0 that means "could not see" from being printed as a
measurement. The alternative word, `partial`, keeps the cells and prints those zeros.
Between erasing a true 786 and printing a false 0, this system's rules pick the first.
The wart underneath is that `worst_coverage` has no way to say "some captures in this
frame saw it and some did not"; see NEXT.

## UNSURE OR NOT DONE

- **The change is untested against a store where it matters, other than by the live run
  and the new integration test.** On all four E16 repositories no row folds from a
  recorder, because all 33 captures that recorded a commit at a rung are attempts. The
  measurement that would change that is the owner's own store after more day-to-day
  capture, and it does not exist yet.
- **A capture that recorded a change an attempt also landed keeps today's dropped
  sentence.** Its columns were not folded (the attempt's were), so the new sentence would
  be false; but "not an attempt of this lineage" is still thinner than what happened. No
  store measured has such a capture, so the third sentence that would name that case is
  not written: it would be prose about a case nobody has seen.
- **The series id of every change-clock frame changes with this task**, whether or not a
  row folds from a recorder, because `series.REDUCER_VERSION` hashes `series.py`,
  `series_lineage.py` and `series_regime.py` and this task edits the second. The direction
  is right and the reason is an accident: `series_changes.py`, `series_outcomes.py` and
  `series_paths.py` are not in that hash, so a change to the change clock's fold alone
  would not have moved it. E16's four ids move: `ser_9179fff7dc0b808ed6a8943c` ->
  `ser_c0c2c8ccf0e830f9542adec6` and the three below it in the table.
- **`env_changed` on a daemon-recorded row is None and will stay None** until a daemon
  capture has an environment fingerprint. Nothing launched the process, so there is no
  argv to fingerprint. It is wave 9's carried item, not this task's.

## NEXT

- `series_lineage.worst_coverage` should be able to say `partial` where two captures of
  one frame disagree, and the cells of a row whose capture could not see a capability
  should be None rather than 0, per row rather than per column. The two go together: the
  first alone prints false zeros and the second alone leaves the word overstated. That is
  a brief of its own, it re-ids every stored series, and it would give the twin row above
  its 786 tokens back with no false 0 beside it.
- `series.REDUCER_VERSION` does not hash `series_changes.py`, `series_outcomes.py` or
  `series_paths.py`, so a change to the change clock's fold alone does not move it. One
  line to fix, and it re-ids every stored series, so it wants its own change.
- The process columns are what H8's next lever needs (E16's own reading). One live row
  exists now. A dozen day-to-day sessions in one repository would make the first
  measurement of whether they forecast anything.

## EXPLAIN FOR THE OWNER

### The row you can look at

W9-T1's live procedure, run once more on this branch on 2026-09-07: a Claude Code
2.1.263 haiku session started by hand in a temporary checkout, with
`telltale daemon --port 47399` listening and no launcher anywhere. It wrote a file,
committed, and said "done" in eight seconds. Telltale recorded 95 observations of it,
which the reducer folded into 9 activities: two model requests, two repository
snapshots, one file edit, one command, one commit and two lifecycle rows. That is the
shape of every session you start yourself. Against `origin/main`, the change clock
printed this for it:

```
files_changed              1
lines_added                1
lines_removed              0
subsystems_touched         1
test_files_changed         0
dependency_delta           0
attempts_to_land           <empty>
fresh_input_tokens_total   <empty>
cache_read_tokens_total    <empty>
compactions                <empty>
verification_cycles        <empty>
edit_turnover_ratio        <empty>
stable_state_intervals     <empty>
unique_files_read          <empty>
```

Six numbers about the commit, and seven blanks about the session that made it. The
session's numbers were in the database the whole time. The clock would not read them,
because a rule written when the launcher was the only recorder said the process half of a
row comes from the ATTEMPT that landed the change, and an attempt is a capture that says
"I am attempt 2 of task T-alpha". A session you start yourself never says that, because
you are working rather than running an orchestration.

After this task, the same row, from the same database:

```
attempts_to_land           <empty>
fresh_input_tokens_total   18
cache_read_tokens_total    37131
compactions                0
verification_cycles        0
edit_turnover_ratio        1.0
stable_state_intervals     1
unique_files_read          0
```

18 fresh input tokens and 37,131 read from cache, no compaction, no verification run, one
file written once, one stable-state interval, no file read. That is the whole of what that
session cost and did, and it is the first row of it anywhere.

### How each of those numbers was measured

None of them is computed by this task. Each is a fold over activities the reducer had
already written, and the change is only WHICH capture's activities the fold reads.

- 18 and 37,131 are sums of `input_tokens` and `cache_read_tokens` over the session's
  `model_request` activities, which come from the OTel `api_request` records Claude Code
  exported to the daemon.
- 0 compactions and 0 verification cycles are counts of activities of those types. They
  are counts and not sums, so 0 is a measurement rather than an unknown: the session
  really had none. The difference matters and the code keeps it: a sum over no rows is
  unknown, a count over no rows is 0.
- 1.0 is edits divided by distinct files edited: it wrote `hello.txt` once.
- 1 stable-state interval is spec 13.5's definition, computed by the one function in the
  codebase that defines an interval, over the repository snapshots the daemon took.
- 0 unique files read is the count of distinct paths on `file_read` activities. The
  session read nothing.

Two are still empty and each says something different. `env_changed` is empty because a
daemon capture has no environment fingerprint at all: a fingerprint is taken of the
process the launcher is about to start, and nothing launched this one. `attempts_to_land`
is empty on purpose, which is the next section.

### The answer that was rejected, and why

The obvious thing to put in `attempts_to_land` for a session like this is 1. It landed the
change, it was one session, so: one attempt.

It is wrong, and it is worth being precise about why, because "1" would look right on
every row and quietly corrupt the column.

`attempts_to_land` does not ask how many sessions this row saw. It asks how many attempts
a change took, and it is a number an orchestrator produces by numbering them: attempt 1
failed review, attempt 2 was reverted, attempt 3 landed, and the row says 3. The rows E16
forecast carry that number because somebody ran `--attempt N` three times.

A session you started by hand numbers nothing. If you tried this change twice and threw
the first away, Telltale would have two captures and no way to know they were about the
same change; if you tried it once, it has one. Both look identical from here. Writing 1
would be this system answering "how many tries" with "how many tries I happened to
record", and a forecaster reading the column could not tell a change that really landed
first time from one whose earlier tries nobody numbered. It would also make the column
mean two different things on two kinds of row in one frame, which is worse than an empty
cell, because an empty cell is excluded by name and a wrong 1 is averaged in.

So it stays empty, the cohort lists the row under `process_from_recording_captures` so you
can find every row where the empty cell means this, and the capture's line in the dropped
table now reads "recorded 46177dc9... at provider_reported: process columns folded,
attempts_to_land unknown" instead of "not an attempt of this lineage".

### What it cost

One thing, and a test caught it. A column's coverage word is one word for the whole frame,
and it is the weakest word any capture folded into that frame recorded. Recorders now join
that fold, so a frame that holds one recorder with no capabilities at all takes its six
capability-backed process columns to `unavailable`, and an unavailable column is emptied,
including on rows an attempt landed. That happens in one fixture in the test suite, where
a synthetic capture delivers no surface, and a row that held 786 tokens now holds nothing.

It is the safe direction rather than merely the briefed one: that same recorder's cells
include counts that are 0 by arithmetic over activities it could never have had, and the
coverage word is what stops a 0 that means "could not see" from being printed as a
measurement. The real fix is that "some captures here saw it and some did not" should read
`partial` and the cells of a capture that could not see a thing should be empty per row,
not per column. That is a change to a function both clocks use, it re-ids every stored
series, and it wants its own brief. It is in NEXT.

On your four E16 repositories this task changes nothing at all, cell for cell, because
every capture in that store that recorded a commit is already an attempt. The store it
changes is the one you started filling last night.
